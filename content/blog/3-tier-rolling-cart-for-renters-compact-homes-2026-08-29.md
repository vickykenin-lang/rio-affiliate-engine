## Real-World Use Cases for Indian Renters

### 1. Kitchen Storage & Mobility
Many Indian kitchens are compact, and renters often lack cabinet space. A 3-tier rolling cart can hold:
- Top tier: Daily-use spices, oils, and small appliances (e.g., mixer-grinder base)
- Middle tier: Cooking essentials (salt, sugar, flour containers)
- Bottom tier: Bulk items (rice, pulses, oil cans)

**Pro tip:** Choose a model with lockable castors if you use it near the stove — safety first.

### 2. Bathroom Organisation
In small Indian homes, the bathroom often doubles as a laundry and storage zone. A rolling cart can hold:
- Top tier: Toiletries and towels
- Middle tier: Cleaning supplies (bottle dispensers, scrubbers)
- Bottom tier: Laundry hamper or folded linens

**Note:** Avoid models with wooden shelves if your bathroom is humid — steel or plastic is more durable.

### 3. Home Office / Study Nook
For renters who work from home or have kids studying in shared spaces, a rolling cart can serve as a mobile desk organiser or supply station:
- Top tier: Pen holder, notebook, calculator
- Middle tier: Stationery, cables, small electronics
- Bottom tier: Extra supplies (paper, binders, chargers)

## How to Choose the Right Model

### Step 1: Measure Your Space
Use a tape measure to confirm:
- Clear width of the doorway or corridor where the cart will pass
- Available floor space (including clearance for opening doors/cupboards)
- Ceiling height (if placing near a fan or light)

### Step 2: Prioritise Mobility or Stability
- **Need to move it daily?** Choose lockable castors and a lighter frame (steel with hollow shelves).
- **Stays in one spot?** Prioritise weight capacity and stability over wheel smoothness.

### Step 3: Check for Renter-Friendly Features
- **No-drill assembly** (tool-free or minimal screws)
- **Removable shelves** (for easy cleaning or reconfiguration)
- **Foldable or collapsible** (for storage when not in use)
